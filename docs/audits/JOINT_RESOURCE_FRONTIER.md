# Complete formation–negativity tradeoff and the remaining memory problem

**Verdict:** the auxiliary two-correlation problem admits an exact joint
formation-entropy/ordinary-negativity frontier, including an explicit phase
boundary and a unique scalar-root construction. The proof below allows
arbitrary finite realization dimension and binary POVMs. This strengthens
the previous incompatibility of separate resource minimizers. It does **not**
evaluate the unrestricted equal-accuracy memory rate or establish publication
originality. A separate real-encoding reduction preserves that open problem
at the asymptotic level, but not at fixed memory or fixed seed size.

## 1. Two different resource questions

The interface still receives one arbitrary unknown n-qubit specimen, encodes
before one delayed local X/Z query, permits unrestricted collective encoding
and unlimited finite classical records, and charges the largest quantum
dimension crossing the boundary in any branch. Accuracy is uniform over
every input, including entangled inputs, and every query. There is no
postselection, physical extra copy, quantum bypass, or preshared entanglement.

Sections 2–5 concern a separate state-realization problem. Let B be a trusted
qubit, let A have arbitrary finite dimension, and choose a state omega_AB
and Hermitian contractions A_0,A_1 with

$$
x=\operatorname{Tr}\omega(A_0\otimes X),\qquad
z=\operatorname{Tr}\omega(A_1\otimes Z),\qquad (x,z)\in[0,1]^2.
\tag{1}
$$

There are no marginal or cross-correlation constraints. Define

$$
E=E_F(\omega)\quad\text{in bits},\qquad
V=2N(\omega)=\|\omega^{T_B}\|_1-1.
\tag{2}
$$

All flags on A count in its realization dimension. This is not the
interface's convention of free classical records. Write

$$
f(v)=h_2\!\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-v^2}}2\right),\quad
\alpha=1/\ln2,\quad q_\lambda(v)=f(v)+\lambda v,
\quad\lambda\ge0.
\tag{3}
$$

The previously evaluated formation minimum is C(x,z). The previous
negativity theorem gives min V=w, where

$$
w=\left[x+z-1-\sqrt{2(1-x)(1-z)}\right]_+.
\tag{4}
$$

Neither C nor V is a proved unrestricted interface memory charge.

## 2. Exact scalarization for every resource price

**Theorem 1.** The minimum of E+lambda V over every realization (1) is
the closed, coordinatewise nondecreasing convex roof C_lambda generated by

$$
(u,s,0),\quad u,s\ge0,\ u^2+s^2\le1;\qquad
(1,v,q_\lambda(v)),\ (v,1,q_\lambda(v)),\quad 0\le v\le1.
\tag{5}
$$

Here convex combinations and decreasing either correlation are allowed.
Dimension three on A suffices for every scalar minimum. C_0=C.

### 2.1 Reduction to qubit blocks does not assume a convex roof for V

Dilate the two contractions to reflections on the same doubled A space,
embedding the state in its original summand. This local isometry preserves
E and V. Jordan's lemma and pinching in the common invariant subspaces then
give a flagged mixture of 1-by-2 and 2-by-2 states, preserving (1) and
decreasing both resources. Both obey the orthogonal-flag identity:

$$
E_F\!\left(\bigoplus_j p_j\tau_j\right)=\sum_jp_jE_F(\tau_j),\qquad
V\!\left(\bigoplus_j p_j\tau_j\right)=\sum_jp_jV(\tau_j).
\tag{6}
$$

For E_F this follows from convexity and local-measurement monotonicity;
for V it follows from the trace norm of a direct sum. On a two-qubit block,

$$
E_F(\tau)\ge f(V(\tau)).
\tag{7}
$$

Indeed, a pure state's entropy is f(c) and its V is c. Convexity of V and
convexity and monotonicity of f give, for every pure decomposition,
sum p f(c)>=f(sum p c)>=f(V(tau)); take the infimum. Ordinary negativity
has not been replaced by convex-roof concurrence in this step.

### 2.2 Sharp mixed-state support bound

Take a>=b>=0, R=sqrt(a^2+b^2). On an irreducible Jordan block, local
rotations give the weighted correlation operator the form
H=s_1 Z tensor Z+s_2 X tensor X, with

$$
s_1\ge s_2\ge0,\qquad s_1^2+s_2^2=R^2,\qquad
s_1+s_2\le a+b,\qquad 0\le s_2\le b.
\tag{8}
$$

The last inequality follows from s_1 s_2<=ab and the ordering.
For its top Bell projector Pi,
H<=(s_1-s_2)I+2s_2 Pi. Partial-transpose trace-norm duality gives
Tr(Pi tau)<=(1+V(tau))/2. Consequently, setting v=V(tau),

$$
\operatorname{Tr}H\tau\le s_1+v s_2\le B_v(a,b),\qquad
B_v(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
R\sqrt{1+v^2},&0\le v\le b/a,\\
a+bv,&b/a\le v\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
$$

The zero-weight case is immediate. Optimize s_2 on [0,b] to obtain (9).
Every B_v is attainable: choose the Alice angle producing the optimal
singular values and a pure Schmidt-rank-two state with correlations (1,v)
in the singular axes. Its resources are exactly (f(v),v). A one-dimensional
block contributes at most R and has zero cost.

It remains to evaluate the scalar support. On the radial branch put
r=sqrt(1+v^2). Both f(sqrt(r^2-1)) and sqrt(r^2-1) are concave in r;
the first is strictly concave. The first assertion is proved explicitly
in [the profile theorem, Eq. (7)](../PRODUCT_DIAGONAL_PROFILE_RATE.md).
Thus Rr-q_lambda(sqrt(r^2-1)) is convex, so its maximum occurs at an
endpoint of the radial interval. With
q_lambda^*(b)=max_{0<=v<=1}[bv-q_lambda(v)], this yields

$$
\max_{0\le v\le1}[B_v(a,b)-q_\lambda(v)]
=\max\{R,\ a+q_\lambda^*(b)\}.
\tag{10}
$$

Extending the axis maximum to all v is legitimate: a+bv<=B_v for every v,
so the added terms cannot exceed the left side. The radial endpoint is
already on that axis branch. Averaging (7)–(10) over the blocks gives

$$
E+\lambda V\ge ax+bz-\max\{R,\ a+q_\lambda^*(b)\}.
\tag{11}
$$

Swap a,b when needed. Supremizing over nonnegative weights is exactly the
convex dual of (5). Conversely, every generator and every flagged mixture
is physical. This proves Theorem 1; Section 3 gives the three-dimensional
attainment, rather than assuming an arbitrary flag count suffices.

## 3. Explicit phase boundary and unique scalar root

Swap x,z so x>=z and define

$$
\tau_\lambda=\frac{2(\alpha-1)^2}{(1+\lambda)^2}.
\tag{12}
$$

**Theorem 2.** The full answer is:

* If x^2+z^2<=1, C_lambda=0.
* If x=1, C_lambda=q_lambda(z).
* If x^2+z^2>1 and x<1, then
  C_lambda=(1+lambda)w exactly when (1-x)/(1-z)>=tau_lambda.
* Otherwise define, for 0<v<=1,

$$
k_\lambda(v)=v-\frac{q_\lambda(v)}{q_\lambda'(v)}
=\frac{vf'(v)-f(v)}{f'(v)+\lambda},\quad
u_\lambda(v)=\frac{1-k_\lambda(v)^2}{1+k_\lambda(v)^2},\quad
s_\lambda(v)=\frac{2k_\lambda(v)}{1+k_\lambda(v)^2}.
\tag{13}
$$

There is a unique root v in (z,1) of

$$
z=v-\frac{(1-x)(v-s_\lambda(v))}{1-u_\lambda(v)}.
\tag{14}
$$

It satisfies u_lambda(v)<x. Set p=(x-u_lambda(v))/(1-u_lambda(v)). Then

$$
(x,z)=(1-p)(u_\lambda,s_\lambda)+p(1,v),\quad
C_\lambda=p[f(v)+\lambda v],\quad
(E_\lambda,V_\lambda)=(pf(v),pv).
\tag{15}
$$

A product state on a one-dimensional Alice flag plus a pure entangled
state on a two-dimensional orthogonal block attains (15). In the central
phase the corresponding pair is (w,w), using the product-plus-Bell
construction. On an exact axis the pair is (f(z),z).

**Proof of the generalization in lambda.** The
[existing contact construction](../PRODUCT_DIAGONAL_PROFILE_RATE.md#51-a-complete-constructive-optimizer-from-one-scalar-root)
extends with the following checks, including the changed endpoint derivative.
The function q_lambda is strictly convex, q_lambda(0)=0,
q_lambda'(0)=lambda, and q_lambda'(1)=alpha+lambda. For v>0,

$$
k_\lambda'(v)=\frac{q_\lambda(v)q_\lambda''(v)}{q_\lambda'(v)^2}>0,
\quad 0<k_\lambda(v)\le k_0(v),\quad
k_\lambda(1)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+\lambda}.
\tag{16}
$$

In particular 0<s_lambda(v)<v and u_lambda(v)>s_lambda(v), as follows
from the lambda=0 inequalities and k_lambda<=k_0. The supporting plane
has coefficients

$$
b=q_\lambda'(v),\quad
a=b\frac{1-k_\lambda^2}{2k_\lambda},\quad
R=b\frac{1+k_\lambda^2}{2k_\lambda}.
\tag{17}
$$

It contacts the free disk at (u_lambda,s_lambda) and the exact-X curve
at (1,v). Strict convexity makes the axis contact unique. For fixed x,
let v_x solve u_lambda(v_x)=x, when it exists. On the physical interval
v>=v_x, p_x=(x-u_lambda)/(1-u_lambda) increases strictly. Thus

$$
Z_x(v)=(1-p_x)s_\lambda+p_xv,\qquad
Z_x'(v)=p_x+(1-p_x)s_\lambda'+p_x'(v-s_\lambda)>0.
\tag{18}
$$

Its endpoints are sqrt(1-x^2) and
1-(1-x)/tau_lambda. The latter follows from
(1-s_lambda(1))/(1-u_lambda(1))=1/tau_lambda. These endpoints give
existence and uniqueness in the strict-saving region. There are no
unphysical roots in (z,1): if u_lambda(v)>x and v>z, then p_x<0 and
Z_x(v)<s_lambda(v)<sqrt(1-x^2)<z. If the physical interval is empty,
the strict-saving region at that x is also empty.

For the central phase, scale the supporting gradient of w by 1+lambda.
Its smaller coefficient is
(1+lambda)[1+sqrt((1-x)/(2(1-z)))]. The Bell contact is optimal exactly
when this coefficient is at least q_lambda'(1)=alpha+lambda. This is
(12). Below that threshold, differentiability of w and the supporting
plane condition exclude equality with (1+lambda)w; (14)–(18) attain the
strictly smaller value. The disk and exact-axis cases follow directly
from (5), with convexity of q_lambda on the axis. QED.

## 4. The entire efficient resource frontier

**Theorem 3.** In the noisy nonclassical interior, define

$$
\lambda_c=\max\left\{0,
\sqrt2(\alpha-1)\sqrt{\frac{1-z}{1-x}}-1\right\}.
\tag{19}
$$

If C=w, the single efficient pair is (E,V)=(w,w). If C<w, the complete
lower Pareto frontier is (E_lambda,V_lambda) from (15) for
0<=lambda<lambda_c, completed by (w,w) at lambda=lambda_c. Here efficient
means no realization has both resources no larger and one strictly smaller.
Along this curve E increases continuously and strictly, and V decreases
continuously and strictly. All V between w and V_0 occur once.

In the strict-saving case C<w, the **minimum E_F under a negativity
budget** V<=V_bar is

$$
\begin{cases}
\text{infeasible},&V_{\rm bar}<w,\\
E_\lambda\text{ at the unique }V_\lambda=V_{\rm bar},
  &w\le V_{\rm bar}\le V_0,\\
C,&V_{\rm bar}\ge V_0.
\end{cases}
\tag{20}
$$

The unique price in (20) lies in [0,lambda_c]. When C=w, the minimum
is w for every V_bar>=w, and lower budgets are infeasible.
The disk has efficient pair (0,0); an exact axis has pair (f(z),z).
Every efficient pair is attained with dim A<=3.

**Proof of completeness.** Form the compact convex hull of generators
with coordinates (x,z,E,V): the free disk with resources (0,0) and the
two exact-axis curves with resources (f(v),v). Include decreasing either
correlation within [0,1]^2. At the specified (x,z), its compact convex resource set H
is physically attained by finite flags. Theorem 1 says that its support
minimum agrees with the minimum over all finite-dimensional realizations
for every nonnegative resource weight (1,lambda). The pure-V limit agrees
as well, by the previous exact negativity theorem.

Every physical pair is coordinatewise dominated by a point of H.
Otherwise separate H from that pair's downward rectangle. The separating
normal is nonnegative and contradicts one of the preceding equal support
minima. This argument does not assume compactness over unbounded Alice
dimension. Every efficient point of H has a nonnegative supporting normal.
Finite lambda gives the unique two-generator resource pair in (15);
the pure-V normal selects (w,w), by the previously proved minimum-E
characterization among V=w states. Thus no extra efficient pairs arise.

Uniqueness of generator contacts gives continuity, including v->1,p->w
as lambda increases to lambda_c. Comparing the optimum inequalities for
two different prices makes V nonincreasing and E nondecreasing. Equality
of V at two prices would force a common optimal generator decomposition.
In the saving phase this is impossible: for a fixed v>0, k_lambda(v)
strictly decreases with lambda, changing the compatible contact point.
Equality of E is excluded similarly, including lambda=0. Hence both
monotonicities are strict. The budget formula follows. Where differentiable,
the slope dE/dV=-lambda is an elementary consequence of scalar optimality.

### A robust consequence of the endpoint

At every noisy interior point the exact endpoint certificate is

$$
E_F+\lambda_c V\ge(1+\lambda_c)w,\qquad
E_F\ge w-\lambda_c(V-w).
\tag{21}
$$

For (x,z)=(0.99,0.5), w=0.39 and
lambda_c=10(1/ln2-1)-1, approximately 3.426950409.
Thus any reduction delta below E_F=0.39 requires an increase at least
delta/(2 lambda_c) above the minimum ordinary negativity N=0.195.
Equation (20) gives the sharp tradeoff beyond this supporting-line bound.

point. On a pure state V equals concurrence. On a mixed two-qubit state
it can be strictly smaller. Therefore the pure-state convex roof of
f(c)+lambda c is generally **not** E_F+lambda V. Simply changing the
axis cost in a prior pure-state proof would not establish Theorem 1.
The mixed-state Bell-projector estimate (9), combined with (7), supplies

## 6. Real encoders: one extra qubit, the same asymptotic rate

**Proposition 4.** For the original real X/Z target effects, let q_C and
q_R be the minimum worst-case integer budgets with arbitrary complex
matrices and with real Kraus/readout matrices respectively. The input in
both definitions is an arbitrary complex quantum state. Then

$$
q_C(n,\eta)\le q_R(n,\eta)\le q_C(n,\eta)+1,
\qquad R_R(\eta)=R_C(\eta).
\tag{22}
$$

The assertion also holds for separate real target effects with the same
uniform error tolerance. To prove it, refine every encoder branch to one
Kraus operator K=A+iC, recording the refinement in the free classical
record. For a Hermitian readout B=P+iQ, put

$$
L=\begin{pmatrix}A\\C\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_R=\begin{pmatrix}P&-Q\\Q&P\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{23}
$$

These are real matrices; B_R has B's spectrum repeated twice. Directly,

$$
L^T B_R L=\operatorname{Re}(K^\dagger B K),\qquad
\sum L^TL=\operatorname{Re}\sum K^\dagger K=I.
\tag{24}
$$

The new instrument has at most twice the output dimension on every branch.
Its effective readout is the entrywise real part of the old one. Exact
real targets are therefore preserved. For an approximate effective
Hermitian readout E and a real target T,
||Re(E)-T||_infinity<=||E-T||_infinity, since real part is the average
of E and its entrywise conjugate. This is precisely the uniform
arbitrary-input error guarantee. No antiunitary is applied to the input;
(23) is an ordinary complex-linear, completely positive instrument with
real matrices. Divide the budget inequality by n to obtain the rate equality.

The underlying dimension-doubling representation is established:
McKague–Mosca–Gisin, [0810.1923v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1923v1),
Section II A–B, Eqs. (1)–(3), printed p. 2, gives the real matrix
representation and explicitly extends it to Kraus operators. The supplied
corollary is the interface-level identity (24) on unchanged unknown inputs
with real target effects, including the worst-case charge. This is not a
claim to have invented real quantum simulation.

### All-size entropy witnesses can also be taken real

For any normalized seed rho, let rho_R=(rho+rho*)/2. Every X_i,Z_i is
real, and root fidelity is jointly concave. Therefore

$$
g_n(\rho_R)\ge g_n(\rho),\qquad
S(\rho_R)\le S(\rho)+1,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}\rho_R\le2\operatorname{rank}\rho.
\tag{25}
$$

Here g_n is the original sum of 2n sandwiched trace norms, equivalently
the root fidelities of rho and P rho P. If a complex seed has positive
excess epsilon=g_n-sqrt(2)n-(2-sqrt(2))S, tensor k copies of that
**mathematical seed**, then take one global real part. Additivity on the
tensor seed and (25) give excess at least
k epsilon-(2-sqrt(2)), positive for sufficiently large k. Hence

$$
\bigl[g_n\le\sqrt2n+(2-\sqrt2)S\text{ for all real seeds and all }n\bigr]
\iff
\bigl[g_n\le\sqrt2n+(2-\sqrt2)S\text{ for all complex seeds and all }n\bigr].
\tag{26}
$$

This is a mathematical reduction between candidate seed inequalities.
The normalized-seed orbit construction would still act on a single
arbitrary nk-qubit specimen. There is no promise of repeated physical
input copies. Neither (22) nor (26) reduces the fixed-n, fixed-budget
problem to real matrices, and neither proves the entropy inequality.

## 7. Decoder provenance and the unresolved entropy step

The preceding report's projective-decoder theorem concerns a two-input
**joint** context, which is weaker than the original independently chosen
local-readout score. For rho>0 its payoff operators are
D_s=Pi_s-Pi_{-s}, c_s=sqrt(rho)D_s sqrt(rho). With
p_t=<t|rho|t> and psi_t=sqrt(rho)|t>/sqrt(p_t), this is discrimination
of four linearly independent pure states with reward
1-d_H(s,t). The ensemble need not be a product ensemble.

Nakahira–Kato–Usuda, [1501.05744v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.05744v2),
Eq. (2) p. 2, Theorem 1/Eqs. (12)–(13) p. 3 and Theorem 2/Eqs. (19)–(20)
p. 4, directly supply the Hermitian-payoff dual and complementarity.
Eldar–Megretski–Verghese,
[quant-ph/0205178v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0205178v1),
Eqs. (24)–(26) p. 13, Eq. (27) and Theorem 2 p. 14, supply the
positive-definite-minus-low-rank kernel method and its projectivity
consequence. These proof tools are prior.

A simple pure-state minimum-error reduction does not settle the supplied
signed-payoff specialization. Indeed, let H be any common shift satisfying
H+D_s>=0 for every s. Opposite signs imply H>=0. If Hv=0, positivity
forces D_{++}v=D_{+-}v=0, impossible for nonzero v because
D_{++}^2+D_{+-}^2=I. Hence H>0, and
H+D_s=(H+Pi_s)-Pi_{-s} has rank at least three. Congruence by sqrt(rho)
preserves rank. Thus every common positive shift gives mixed hypotheses
of rank at least three, not the rank-one hypotheses of that old theorem.
This excludes a specific subsumption route, not every possible reduction.

A particularly close source remains unread at theorem level:
Nakahira–Usuda, *Minimum-Bayes-cost discrimination for symmetric quantum
states*, [PRA 86, 062305 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.86.062305).
Its primary abstract announces a sufficient rank condition for optimal
Bayes measurements. Public retrieval did not supply the full theorem.
Its relation to the signed-payoff argument is an explicit originality gap.

Neither projectivity nor the new joint resource frontier bounds the
entropy of a general two-qubit seed. The next exact local target remains

$$
\max_\rho\left[g_2(\rho)-(2-\sqrt2)S(\rho)\right]\stackrel{?}=2\sqrt2.
\tag{27}
$$

No all-state proof or admissible violating seed was obtained in this
continuation. A full asymmetric-profile monogamy theorem would imply
(27), but is stronger than needed. In the weighted-conjugate language,
only the common weight a=b=1/(2-sqrt(2)) is needed for this first open
case. Standard split-party strong-superadditivity arguments cannot simply
be applied: both trusted sites share the same unrestricted Alice system.
Kim, [2101.05590v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05590v1), Theorem 1,
Eq. (33) p. 8, requires additional ccq mutual-information identities;
trusted qubits alone do not furnish them. The general conjugate framework
in Audenaert–Braunstein, [CMP 246, 443–452 (2004)](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~sb54/papers/ab04.pdf),
Lemma 1/Eqs. (11)–(12) p. 447 and Theorem 1/Eqs. (15)–(16) p. 448,
does not remove that shared-system obstruction.

## 8. Verification and publication status

The mixed-block estimate, support elimination, all-lambda contact proof,
boundary cases, and Pareto completeness were reconstructed analytically
and checked in separate workspace derivations. The real Kraus identity,
uniform complex-input error guarantee, and all-size witness reduction
were independently checked. Primary PDFs were inspected for the source
maps above; the expressly identified 2012 source is an abstract-only gap.
The small arithmetic example illustrates an exact formula and is not a
numerical proof. No large simulation, new solver certificate, or global
search claim supports these theorems. Unchanged diagnostic scripts were
not rerun. Editorial checks cover local links, whitespace and protected
file identity.

**Established ingredients:** Jordan reduction, entanglement monotonicity
and flags, pure weighted steering geometry, concurrence/negativity
relations, convex duality, realification, and general decision-theory SDP.

**Supplied deductions:** the arbitrary-dimensional E_F/V scalar and budget
evaluation (5)–(20), its quantitative endpoint consequence, and the exact
interface/error and all-size entropy consequences of realification.

**Unresolved:** unrestricted equal-accuracy optimality, an all-state proof
of (27), the new theorem's historical priority and publication merit, and
the stated decoder-rank source comparison. No claim has moved from this
last category merely because the auxiliary optimization is now explicit.
