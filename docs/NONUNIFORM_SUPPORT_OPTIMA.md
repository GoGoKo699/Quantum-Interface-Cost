# Exact nonuniform optima on small fixed supports

Date: 22 September 2026. Research base:
`6ddc97a549316ed9edb7a14a110ceee70b87bdf9`.

**Status:** supplied analytical deductions, independently reconstructed within
this workspace. These examples refute two
possible spectrum-flattening proof steps. They do not violate the subset
benchmark or the seed entropy conjecture, and publication novelty is not
claimed. The optimizations below allow every density matrix on each stated
support, including matrices with coherences in the displayed basis.

For a density matrix rho on n qubits, use the score

$$
g(\sqrt\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{U=X_i,Z_i}
\|\sqrt\rho\,U\sqrt\rho\|_1.
\tag{1}
$$

The [normalized-seed reduction](COLLECTIVE_ENCODING_REDUCTION.md) allows
every Gram matrix rho of the permitted rank. Replacing rho by its normalized
support projector is therefore a substantive optimization claim, rather
than a harmless normalization. Both examples below show why that step is
unavailable.

## 1. Concavity and symmetries give exact fixed-support reductions

Let

$$
B=(X+Z)/\sqrt2,
$$

and write `|0_B>`, `|1_B>` for its positive and negative eigenstates.
Conjugation by B exchanges X and Z. Consequently the score is invariant
under every local B_i, and also under permutations of the input sites.

The score is concave in rho. To give the needed reason explicitly, each
summand is root fidelity:

$$
\|\sqrt\rho U\sqrt\rho\|_1
=F(\rho,U\rho U),\qquad
F(A,C)=\|\sqrt A\sqrt C\|_1.
$$

Root fidelity has the variational expression

$$
F(A,C)=\frac12\inf_{H>0}
\bigl[\operatorname{Tr}(AH)+\operatorname{Tr}(CH^{-1})\bigr].
\tag{2}
$$

For completeness, trace-norm duality and Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz
give `F(A,C) <= sqrt(Tr(AH) Tr(CH^{-1}))`, which is at most the
displayed arithmetic mean. For positive definite A and C, equality is
attained at

$$
H=A^{-1/2}(A^{1/2}CA^{1/2})^{1/2}A^{-1/2},
$$

since `HAH=C` and both traces equal F. For singular A or C, approximate
them by `A+epsilon I`, `C+epsilon I`: the lower bound still holds, while
using a minimizer of the positive definite problem bounds the infimum for
A,C above by `F(A+epsilon I,C+epsilon I)`. Continuity gives (2).
The infimum of linear functions of `(A,C)` is concave, proving the claim.

Now take a support spanned by selected product-bisector basis vectors. It
is invariant under the B_i. Averaging rho over the group generated by the
B_i preserves its support and dephases it in that product basis. Concavity
and invariance show that this averaging cannot decrease g. If the support
is also permutation invariant, averaging site permutations cannot decrease
g either. Thus the maximum on either support below is attained by a
diagonal state whose nonzero weights have the stated permutation symmetry.
This proves the reductions over **all** states on those supports; diagonal
states have not been assumed at the start.

We will use the elementary two-weight formula from the
[product-diagonal proof](COMMUTING_SEED_BOUND.md). On an edge of the
product-bisector basis with probabilities p and r, either local query has
the compressed matrix, up to signs,

$$
\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}p&\sqrt{pr}\\ \sqrt{pr}&-r\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its determinant is `-pr`, and its trace norm is

$$
\frac1{\sqrt2}\sqrt{p^2+6pr+r^2}.
\tag{3}
$$

The formula includes a zero weight. Summing over orthogonal edge blocks
gives the full trace norm of each query.

## 2. A rank-three two-qubit support

Define

$$
V_2=\operatorname{span}\{|00_B\rangle,|10_B\rangle,|01_B\rangle\}.
$$

**Proposition 1.** The exact fixed-support optimum is

$$
\boxed{\max_{\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq V_2}
g(\sqrt\rho)=\frac{18\sqrt2}{7}.}
\tag{4}
$$

It is attained by the diagonal state with probabilities

$$
p(00)=\frac37,\qquad p(10)=p(01)=\frac27.
\tag{5}
$$

Indeed, Section 1 reduces the optimization to
`p(00)=a`, `p(10)=p(01)=b=(1-a)/2`. At every site, one edge has weights
(a,b), and the other has weights (b,0). Equation (3) therefore gives

$$
g_2(a)=2\sqrt2\bigl[\sqrt{a^2+6ab+b^2}+b\bigr]
=\sqrt2\bigl[\sqrt{1+10a-7a^2}+1-a\bigr].
\tag{6}
$$

For `0<a<1`, the derivative divided by `sqrt(2)` is

$$
\frac{5-7a}{\sqrt{1+10a-7a^2}}-1.
$$

When `a>=5/7` it is negative. For `a<5/7`, both quantities being compared
are nonnegative, and

$$
(5-7a)^2-(1+10a-7a^2)
=8(7a-3)(a-1).
$$

Thus the derivative is positive for `a<3/7` and negative for `a>3/7`.
Substitution proves (4)--(5), including the endpoints by continuity.

### This exceeds every flat rank-three two-qubit score

Every rank-three projector on two qubits has the form
`P=I-|v><v|`. For any local X or Z query U, its compression to the
three-dimensional range of P has eigenvalues

$$
1,\quad -1,\quad-\langle v|U|v\rangle.
$$

The first two eigenvalues follow because each two-dimensional eigenspace
of U intersects the range of P in at least one dimension; the third follows
from the trace. Consequently

$$
g(\sqrt{P/3})=
\frac{8+\sum_{i=1}^2
(|\langle X_i\rangle_v|+|\langle Z_i\rangle_v|)}3
\le\frac{8+2\sqrt2}{3}.
\tag{7}
$$

Each reduced Bloch vector has length at most one, proving the inequality.
A product-bisector choice of v attains it. This independently reproduces
the exact flat-rank-three value from the
[entropy-boundary note](ENTROPY_INEQUALITY_BOUNDARIES.md).

The strict comparison is

$$
\frac{18\sqrt2}{7}-\frac{8+2\sqrt2}{3}
=\frac{8(5\sqrt2-7)}{21}>0.
\tag{8}
$$

Thus the following assertion is false: **at each fixed rank r, the global
score maximum can be attained by a flat rank-r state.** Rank three on two
qubits already refutes it. The example does not establish the unrestricted
rank-three optimum, nor refute a possible global statement restricted to
power-of-two ranks.

## 3. A rank-four three-qubit support

Define

$$
V_3=\operatorname{span}\{
|000_B\rangle,|100_B\rangle,|010_B\rangle,|001_B\rangle\}.
$$

**Proposition 2.** The exact fixed-support optimum is

$$
\boxed{\max_{\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq V_3}
g(\sqrt\rho)=3\sqrt3.}
\tag{9}
$$

An attaining state has probabilities

$$
p(000)=1-\frac{\sqrt6}{4},\qquad
p(100)=p(010)=p(001)=\frac{\sqrt6}{12}.
\tag{10}
$$

Section 1 reduces the optimization to a central weight a and three equal
weights `b=(1-a)/3`. At each site the nonzero edge blocks have weights
(a,b), (b,0), and (b,0). Equation (3) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_3(a)
&=3\sqrt2\bigl[\sqrt{a^2+6ab+b^2}+2b\bigr]\\
&=\sqrt2\bigl[\sqrt{1+16a-8a^2}+2(1-a)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$

The derivative divided by `sqrt(2)` is

$$
\frac{8(1-a)}{\sqrt{1+16a-8a^2}}-2.
$$

Its sign is that of

$$
16(1-a)^2-(1+16a-8a^2)
=24(1-a)^2-9,
$$

since both sides in the unsquared comparison are nonnegative. It changes
from positive to negative at `a=1-sqrt(6)/4`. At this point
`sqrt(1+16a-8a^2)=sqrt(6)`, so (11) gives `3sqrt(3)`.
All six individual query scores equal `sqrt(3)/2`.

In contrast, the normalized projector onto this same support has
`a=b=1/4`, and hence

$$
g(\sqrt{P_{V_3}/4})=3+\frac32\sqrt2<3\sqrt3.
\tag{12}
$$

The strict inequality follows from `2sqrt(3)>2+sqrt(2)`, whose square
reduces to `3>2sqrt(2)`. Therefore **replacing a seed by the normalized
projector onto its own support cannot generally improve its score**, even
when that support has power-of-two dimension four and lies in the precise
unresolved block `n=3,q=2`.

This is a statement about a fixed support. It does not exceed the global
flat-rank-four optimum:

$$
3\sqrt3<4+\sqrt2.
$$

For example, squaring this last comparison reduces to `9<8sqrt(2)`.
The [flat half-rank theorem](FLAT_HALF_RANK_OPTIMALITY.md) and the
unrestricted nonflat problem therefore remain fully consistent with (9).

## 4. Consequences for the research target

The two invalid steps are distinct:

| Proposed step | Exact obstruction |
|---|---|
| Flatten a state on its existing support without decreasing its score. | False already on V_2, and also on the power-of-two support V_3. |
| Optimize globally at rank r by considering only flat rank-r states. | False at n=2, r=3, because (4) exceeds the global flat value (7). |

Neither example refutes global flat optimality specifically at power-of-two
ranks. In particular, neither settles `Gamma(3,4) <= 4+sqrt(2)`.
Both attaining states are product diagonal and consequently obey the
already proved seed entropy inequality. No collective advantage, improved
unrestricted rate, or unrestricted nonflat optimum is claimed.

The supports and spectra in this note are mathematical Gram matrices in
the existing seed optimization. The original task still has one unknown
specimen, one delayed query, arbitrary collective encoding, unrestricted
finite classical records, worst-case quantum dimension, and uniform
arbitrary-input statistics. Operational achievability uses the complete
seed orbit from the reduction; the seed alone is not a postselected
physical protocol.
